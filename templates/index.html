Study Overview

This application provides access to RNA-seq gene expression data from a mouse study examining the functional impact of Apoer2 (Lrp8) exon 19 splicing in the context of ApoE3 and ApoE4 genotypes.


APOE is one of the strongest genetic risk factors for Alzheimer's disease. The APOE4 allele is overrepresented in Alzheimer's patients (40-60% of cases) and interacts with LDL receptors in the brain differently from APOE3. This study investigates how alternative splicing of exon 19 in the Apoer2 receptor modulates downstream transcriptomic responses in each genotype.

Experimental Design

  • Species: Mus musculus, male mice only
  • ApoE Genotypes: e3/e3 (ApoE3) and e4/e4 (ApoE4)
  • Exon 19 Status: Present (+ex19) or Deleted (Δex19)
  • Conditions: a3c, a3d, a4c, a4d encoding genotype × exon combinations
  • 4 experimental groups from the 2×2 factorial design

Key Questions Addressed

  • Top 20 differentially expressed genes in ApoE4 vs. ApoE3
  • Mean raw counts per genotype for any specific gene
  • Genes DE between ApoE3 and ApoE4 with exon 19 present
  • How exon 19 deletion alters the transcriptomic response
  • Condition-specific expression profiles across all 4 groups

DE Statistics Glossary

The differential expression tables report the following statistics for each gene. Click any column header in a results table to jump directly to its definition here.

logFC Log₂ Fold Change

The log₂ ratio of mean expression between the two conditions being compared. A value of +1 means the gene is expressed 2× higher in condition A vs. B; −1 means 2× lower. Positive values indicate up-regulation in the first-listed condition; negative values indicate down-regulation. Larger absolute values reflect stronger differential expression.

logCPM Log₂ Counts Per Million

The average log₂ expression level of the gene across all samples, expressed as counts per million (CPM) mapped reads. CPM normalizes for sequencing depth so that samples with different total read counts can be compared. Higher logCPM values indicate more highly expressed genes. This metric is useful for gauging baseline expression abundance — very low logCPM values (e.g. below 0) may suggest the gene is lowly expressed and DE calls should be interpreted with extra caution.

F F-Statistic (quasi-likelihood)

The F-statistic from the quasi-likelihood F-test implemented in edgeR. It measures the ratio of the variance explained by the difference between groups to the residual (within-group) variance. A larger F value indicates a stronger signal of differential expression relative to the noise in the data. The F-test is preferred over the likelihood ratio test when sample sizes are small because it better accounts for uncertainty in the dispersion estimate.

P-Value Nominal p-value

The raw (unadjusted) probability of observing an F-statistic at least as extreme as the one calculated, assuming no true differential expression (null hypothesis). Small p-values suggest the observed difference is unlikely to be due to chance alone. However, because thousands of genes are tested simultaneously, the raw p-value alone is not sufficient for declaring significance — use the FDR (adjusted p-value) for filtering instead.

FDR False Discovery Rate (Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted p-value)

The p-value corrected for multiple testing using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure. When testing thousands of genes at once, many will appear significant by chance. The FDR represents the expected proportion of false positives among all genes called significant at that threshold. An FDR of 0.05 means that approximately 5% of genes passing that cutoff are expected to be false positives. This is the primary metric used for filtering results in this application. Color coding: green ≤ 0.001, amber ≤ 0.05, grey > 0.05.

Biological Glossary

  • Alzheimer's Disease (AD): A progressive neurodegenerative disorder and the most common cause of dementia, characterized by memory loss, mood disturbances, and decline of executive function. Hallmarks include amyloid-β plaques and neurofibrillary tau tangles.
  • Sporadic AD (sAD): The most prevalent form (~95% of cases), arising without a single causative mutation. Risk is driven by age and genetic factors such as the APOE4 allele.
  • Familial AD: A rare inherited form (~5% of cases) caused by highly penetrant mutations, typically in APP, PSEN1, or PSEN2.
  • APOE (Apolipoprotein E): A lipoprotein produced predominantly by astrocytes in the CNS that mediates lipid transport, immune modulation, and signal transduction by binding members of the LDLR family.
  • APOE4 (ε4 allele): The strongest genetic risk factor for sporadic AD, increasing risk 3–4 fold. Present in ~14% of the general population but 40–65% of AD patients.
  • APOE3 (ε3 allele): The most common APOE isoform (~79% of the population), considered the neutral reference allele for AD risk comparisons.
  • APOER2 / LRP8: A brain-predominant LDLR family member that regulates neuronal migration, dendritic spine and synapse formation, and transcription of learning and memory genes. Binds ApoE, Reelin, and clusterin. Highly alternatively spliced in neurons.
  • Exon 19 (Ex19): An alternatively spliced exon of Apoer2 whose inclusion (+Ex19) or exclusion (ΔEx19) alters APOER2 signaling and produces distinct hippocampal transcriptomic profiles, with effects differing between APOE3 and APOE4 backgrounds.
  • Knock-in (KI): A genetic modification inserting a specific allele (here, human APOE3 or APOE4) at the endogenous mouse locus to model human gene function.
  • RNA-seq: A high-throughput technique that quantifies genome-wide gene expression by sequencing RNA transcripts. Used here to profile hippocampal transcriptomes across four mouse genotypes.
  • Differential Expression (DE): A statistical comparison of gene expression between two conditions. Significantly different genes are called differentially expressed genes (DEGs).
  • PCA (Principal Component Analysis): Summarizes genome-wide expression into a few components. Samples with similar profiles cluster together.
  • GO (Gene Ontology): Standardized vocabulary for gene function across Biological Process, Cellular Component, and Molecular Function. Used to identify functional themes among DEGs.
  • GSEA / NES: Gene Set Enrichment Analysis tests whether a gene set (e.g. a GO pathway) is enriched among the most up- or down-regulated genes. The Normalized Enrichment Score (NES) reflects direction and magnitude.
  • Amyloid-β (Aβ): A peptide that aggregates into extracellular plaques in the brain; a hallmark of Alzheimer's pathology.
  • Tau / Neurofibrillary Tangles: Tau is a microtubule-associated protein that forms intracellular tangles when hyperphosphorylated — a second Alzheimer's hallmark.
  • Reelin: A secreted glycoprotein and APOER2 ligand that regulates neuronal migration and synaptic plasticity.
  • Hippocampus: A brain region critical for learning and memory, vulnerable early in Alzheimer's disease. Tissue here is male mouse hippocampus harvested at 3 months.
